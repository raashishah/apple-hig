# Design principles

### design-principles.001
Value comes first. Strong work keeps asking, through development, what the product exists to do and whether each choice supports that aim.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.002
Narrow the effort. Spend it on the features people rely on most and make those excellent, because a clear job helps them reach the goals they bring.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.003
Look for a new approach to the problem: examine what already exists, refuse to rebuild it, name what makes the product different, and ask whether the design shows that difference.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.004
Don’t get in the way: people arrive to finish a job or reach material, and the strongest help is a direct route that stays quiet until it’s needed.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.005
Leave room to wander. People should reach features without being trapped in a single path or mode, and when guidance is required they should be able to skip it and arrive at the main experience fast.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.006
Make mistakes recoverable. If an action can be undone or an earlier state restored, exploration feels safe and the UI feels welcoming; build that forgiveness in so recovery doesn’t spend their time or their work.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.007
Say plainly what the product does and why, starting with the first interaction. Explain each permission request, and when data is gathered, say what is taken and how it is used.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.008
Protect what people share. Collect only what the product must have, handle it carefully, and anticipate misuse or harm so safeguards exist before abuse or unintended consequences.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.009
Build on ideas people already understand, from everyday life and from other software, so the UI feels known and intuitive.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.010
Repeat visual choices and interaction choices once you set them. That steadiness helps people learn faster and trust that an unfamiliar control will behave as they assume.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.011
Tell people what is happening: mark which controls can be used, show when material changes, and follow platform patterns for alerts and choices so they stay informed and in control.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.012
Design so everyone can take part. Account for a wide range of people, experiences, perspectives, and needs, treat accessibility as a starting requirement, and aim the result at the widest audience.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.013
Keep a person’s place in the experience as the design moves across platforms and configurations: hold material and controls in predictable spots, and let natural animation soften the transitions.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.014
Plan for many ways of providing input. Voice, touch, keyboard, and other methods should all be able to drive the product, because people don’t all work the same way.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.015
Treat every platform as a first-class home. The software should feel finished wherever it runs, with equal care for each one you ship.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.016
Keep only what is needed. That is not the same as stripping things to a minimum; hold the important pieces close and let the rest recede.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.017
Say it in the fewest words that still carry the idea. Short phrasing is often the version more people understand, so pick exactly the words a label or a concept requires.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.018
Make hierarchy obvious. When shape and purpose are easy to see, people can tell where they stand and what happens next; favor controls they recognize and a structure that stays steady.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.019
Quality is the tone. Each choice should be deliberate: visuals that impress, motion that is smooth, words that are exact, and sound that is considered.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.020
Try things and revise. Prototype soon, test fresh approaches, throw away what fails, hold every feature to a high standard, and check the product in real conditions so it stays durable, reliable, and fast.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.021
Craft continues after you ship. Keep the UI aligned with current platform capabilities and patterns, and don’t lower the quality standard; design stays an open commitment.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.022
Name the feeling you want people to have. Fitness might energize, meditation might calm, and play might excite; let that feeling guide the design.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.023
Make moments that define the product. Even a button press or an error can express what the software stands for, so ask whether that moment can carry a little character.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.024
Don’t mistake delight for decoration. People are trying to finish a task, so chasing delight for its own sake shouldn’t block the product’s core purpose. Consider the overall aesthetic: some designs want a carefully considered practical touch, others a bit of whimsy, and experimenting finds the balance.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles

### design-principles.025
Look at the whole experience. Delight is everything added together: freedom to act, room to explore safely, metaphors that feel known, and the ability to move between contexts. Intent, focus, and care produce something people experience as naturally delightful.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles
