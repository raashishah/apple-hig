# Lists and tables

### lists-and-tables.001
Text belongs in a list or a table more often than other content. Rows make wording easy to scan, even though a table may hold anything; when item sizes differ a lot, or many pictures must appear, look at a different container.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.002
Allow table editing when it belongs in the app. Reordering is welcome even when adding and removing aren’t, and iOS and iPadOS require an editing mode before table items can be selected.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.003
Selection feedback should match the result, whether the choice reveals another view or toggles state. Navigation through a hierarchy generally keeps the chosen row highlighted so the path stays clear, whereas an options table usually flashes the row and then adds a mark such as a checkmark to show selection.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.004
Keep item text short so a row is comfortable to read. Brief wording reduces truncation and wrapping and makes scanning easier; if an item is a large block of text, list titles only and let a choice reveal the rest in a detail view.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.005
Plan for text that might clip or truncate so it stays readable. In a narrow table, including one people can resize, content should remain recognizable; a middle ellipsis can distinguish an item by keeping its opening and its closing.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.006
In a multicolumn table, use descriptive headings made of nouns or brief noun phrases, and don’t add ending punctuation. A single-column table can skip a column heading if a label or a header still explains the context.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.007
Match the list or table style to the data and the platform. Visual details in some styles communicate grouping, hierarchy, or a particular experience: grouped style on iOS and iPadOS separates data groups using headers, footers, and added space; the elliptical style on watchOS makes items look as though they roll off a rounded surface during scrolling; macOS bordered style alternates row backgrounds so large tables are easier to use.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.008
Pick a row style suited to the information, such as a small image at the leading side followed by a short explanatory label. Some platforms include built-in row styles for list content, and one API lays out rows, headers, and footers on iOS, iPadOS, and tvOS.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.009
On iOS, iPadOS, and visionOS, an info button should only reveal more about what the row contains. Inside a list row it is called a detail disclosure button, and it is not for moving through a hierarchy; drilling into subviews calls for a disclosure indicator accessory instead.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.010
On iOS, iPadOS, and visionOS, skip an alphabetical index when trailing-edge controls such as disclosure indicators are already in the rows. The index usually runs down the trailing side, and a letter jumps to its section; sharing that edge with the indicators makes it easy to hit one while aiming for the other.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.011
On macOS, when it helps, let a click on a column heading sort the table by that column. If that column is already the sort, sort again in the opposite direction.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.012
On macOS, allow column resizing. Widths in a table often differ, and people like adjusting them to focus elsewhere or to uncover data that was cut off.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.013
On macOS, alternating colors on rows can help in a table with several columns. People can follow values across a row more easily, particularly when the table is wide.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.014
On macOS, use an outline view rather than a table for hierarchical data. It resembles a table but adds disclosure triangles that expose nested levels, such as folders and the items inside them.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.015
On tvOS, images beside a table should still look right when focus highlights a row and enlarges it a little. Corners of a focused row may round, which can change pictures on either side, so prepare artwork for that and avoid adding masks of your own to round those corners.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.016
On watchOS, keep row counts low when possible, because short lists scan more easily, though a long one is sometimes what people expect — a large podcast library that can’t be seen in full may look broken. Show the most relevant entries and offer a path to the rest so a long list stays manageable.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables

### lists-and-tables.017
On watchOS, detail views must stay short to support vertical page-based navigation, where a vertical swipe moves among detail items from different rows and skips the return to the list. Scrolling detail views disable that swipe between them.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables
