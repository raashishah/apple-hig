# Motion

### motion.001
Motion should support the experience, not take it over. Animation with no purpose, or too much of it, distracts people and can leave them feeling disconnected or physically uncomfortable.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.002
Keep motion optional, because not everyone can experience it or wants to. Never let it be the sole channel for important information, and pair what people see with other cues.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.003
Feedback should feel realistic and follow the gesture people just made. Outside games, accurate movement explains how something works, while movement that fights expectations can disorient them; a view revealed by sliding downward from the top should not leave by sliding sideways.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.004
Short, exact feedback animations feel light and stay out of the way, and they often communicate better than a big one. A game can tie a brief animation tightly to a success so players understand it without leaving play; in visionOS, tapping a panorama in Photos makes it expand quickly and smoothly until it fills the space ahead of them.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.005
In apps, skip extra motion on interface actions that happen all the time. Standard elements already get subtle system animation, and a custom element should not ask for attention on every use.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.006
People should be able to cancel motion. Do not make them wait out an animation before they can act, especially when they would see it again.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.007
Animated symbols are worth using where they fit. SF Symbols 5 and newer let you animate both SF Symbols and symbols you create.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.008
Ship motion that already looks good on every platform you support. Holding a frame rate from 30 up to 60 fps usually looks smooth and appealing, and each device’s graphics should set defaults people can enjoy before they change anything.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.009
Let players tune visuals toward performance or toward battery life. When the system sees an external power source, for example, offer a switch between power modes.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.010
On visionOS, keep motion away from the edges of someone’s field of view. Movement in peripheral vision is especially distracting and can cause discomfort, because it suggests the person or the room is moving; if an object must travel out there during an immersive experience, match its brightness to the rest of what is visible.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.011
On visionOS, large virtual objects need extra care when they move. Something big enough to fill much of the view and hide most or all of the surroundings can feel like part of the room; make it more translucent so people see through it, or drop its contrast so the travel is easier to ignore, and they are less likely to feel that they or the room are moving.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.012
On visionOS, a fade is a good way to relocate an object. People naturally watch travel, so if the trip tells them nothing, fade the object out, move it, then fade it in at the destination.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.013
On visionOS, do not generally let people spin a virtual world. Even a subtle spin they control tends to upset their sense of stability; prefer an instant change of direction inside a quick fade-out.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.014
On visionOS, a stationary frame of reference helps. Movement is easier to handle inside an area that stays put; if the whole surrounding area seems to travel — a game that carries a player through space on its own, for example — people can feel unwell.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion

### motion.015
On visionOS, avoid objects that keep oscillating, especially near a frequency of 0.2 Hz, which many people find hard to tolerate. If you must show oscillation, hold the amplitude down and consider translucent content.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion
