# Tab bars

### tab-bars.001
Use a tab bar to move between areas of the app, the way Clock separates Alarm, Stopwatch, and Timer, and keep actions that change the current view out of it.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.002
Keep the tab bar visible as people move among sections. Hiding it makes them forget where they are. A modal view may cover it, because a modal is temporary and self-contained.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.003
Use as many tabs as navigation needs, and no more. Tabs represent the hierarchy, so weigh the cost of extra tabs against how often each section is needed; fewer tabs are generally easier to move among. For a complex information structure, consider a sidebar, or a tab bar that adapts into a sidebar, where that’s available.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.004
Avoid overflow: size and orientation can display fewer tabs than exist, and when horizontal room runs out the last tab turns into More on iOS and iPadOS, listing the rest separately, which makes hidden sections harder to reach and to notice, so keep that situation rare.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.005
Don’t disable or hide tab bar buttons, even when their content is unavailable. Buttons that sometimes vanish make the interface feel unstable and unpredictable. If a section is empty, explain why.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.006
Add a label under or beside each icon so the section’s content or role is obvious, and prefer a single word.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.007
Consider SF Symbols for familiar, scalable tab icons. With SF Symbols, icons adapt automatically — the bar can be regular or compact depending on device and orientation, with icons above labels in compact views and side by side in regular views. Prefer filled symbols or icons to match the platform.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.008
Use a badge when critical information is waiting. A badge is a red oval with white text, either a number or an exclamation point, signaling new or updated information that deserves attention. Reserve badges for critical news so they keep their impact.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.009
Don’t give tab labels a color close to the content-layer background. If the content layer is already bright and colorful, prefer a monochromatic tab bar, or an accent color with enough visual separation.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.010
On iPadOS, prefer a tab bar for navigation so people can reach the sections they use most. If your app is more complex, let them convert the tab bar into a sidebar for a wider set of options.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.011
Let people customize the tab bar when many sections might matter. They can add frequent items and remove infrequent ones — in Music, someone can pin a favorite playlist. If you allow custom tabs, aim for a built-in list of five or fewer so compact and regular sizes stay consistent.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.012
You can specify a tint, a color, or an image for the tab bar background.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.013
You can choose a font for tab items, including a different font for the selected item.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.014
You can specify tints for selected items and for unselected items.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.015
You can add button icons, such as settings and search.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.016
Account for how the tab bar scrolls. By default it can leave the screen when the current tab holds a single main view, as in the TV app tabs Kids, Sports, TV Show, Movies, and Watch Now. A split view is the exception, such as the TV app’s Library tab or a Settings screen, where the bar stays pinned at the top while content scrolls in the primary and secondary panes. Whatever the tab contains, pressing Menu on the remote returns focus to the tab bar at the top of the page.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.017
In a live-viewing app, organize tabs consistently. For live-streaming apps, use the order in the items that follow.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.018
Put live content first.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.019
Next, offer Cloud DVR or other recorded content.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.020
Then offer any remaining content.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.021
Give every tab a symbol and a text label. The symbol stays visible; looking at the bar reveals the labels as well. Even though the bar expands, keep labels short enough to read at a glance.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars

### tab-bars.022
If it fits, consider a sidebar inside a tab for secondary navigation when the hierarchy is deep. Selections in that sidebar must not change which tab is open.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars
