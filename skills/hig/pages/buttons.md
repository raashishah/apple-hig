# Buttons

### buttons.001
Style is a look that comes from size, color, and shape.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.002
Content is a symbol, a text label, or both, and it communicates what the button does.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.003
Role is a meaning defined by the system that can also affect appearance.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.004
Leave enough room around a button that people can tell it apart from nearby components and content, and so they can activate it with whatever input they use. The hit region should be at least 44x44 pt, or 60x60 pt in visionOS, so a fingertip, pointer, gaze, or remote can select it comfortably.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.005
Always give a custom button a pressed appearance. Missing that state makes the control feel unresponsive and leaves people unsure their input was accepted.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.006
Save the strongest visual treatment for the action a screen most expects, and let the system apply an accent tint to that background. Color is what people notice first, so cap standout controls at a couple in each screen; a crowd of them increases cognitive load and delays the choice.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.007
Separate the preferred choice from the others by style rather than by size. Equal sizes imply one coherent set, while two different sizes side by side look muddled, so strengthen the style of the likely option and quiet the rest.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.008
Do not color a button label so it nearly matches the content-layer background. If that layer is already vivid, keep the default single-color label treatment.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.009
Make the purpose of every button obvious. A platform may allow a symbol, a written label, or the two together.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.010
Link well-known actions to well-known icons, such as square.and.arrow.up for sharing. When an icon fits, prefer one that already exists or a customized variant of one.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.011
Choose a short written label when it says more than an icon would, in a few words that describe the action. A verb-first label helps, for instance Add to Cart on a control that puts items into a shopping cart.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.012
Normal carries no particular meaning.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.013
Primary identifies the default choice, the one someone will probably pick.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.014
Cancel means the button abandons the action now in progress.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.015
Destructive means the action can destroy data.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.016
Assign primary to the control someone will probably activate. If it responds to Return, confirmation is quick, and inside a temporary surface such as an editable view the same role can dismiss that surface when Return is pressed.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.017
Keep the primary role off any action that destroys data, even when that action is the probable choice. People sometimes hit a prominent button before reading it, so reserve the role for actions that preserve content.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.018
If an action will not finish at once, put an activity indicator in the button to explain the wait without spending extra space. The label can change while that indicator shows, for instance from Checkout to Checking out…, and after a click or tap the system shows the indicator beside the original or replacement label and hides the button image if one exists.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.019
Reach for a variable-height push button only to hold content that is tall or changes height. It shares setup options, rounded corners, and padding with an ordinary push button, so pick it when you must show two lines of lettering or a tall symbol, and otherwise keep a standard push button.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.020
End the title with an ellipsis if activating the push button will open a further window, view, or app. Systemwide, that punctuation means people can supply more input, which is why Edit controls in Safari Settings’ AutoFill pane include it: they open other views where autofill values can be modified.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.021
Consider spring loading. With a Magic Trackpad, dragging selected items over a button and force clicking — pressing harder — can activate it without dropping those items, after which dragging can continue for further actions.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.022
Keep square buttons inside a view, outside the window frame. They do not belong in a toolbar or a status bar; a toolbar button should be a toolbar item.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.023
Prefer a symbol on a square button so it can pick up appropriate color by default and again when someone interacts.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.024
Skip introductory labels on square buttons. Their close tie to one view usually makes a written description unnecessary.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.025
Open help documentation with the help button the system supplies. Its familiar look tells people that help content will appear.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.026
Open the help topic tied to the current context when you can. In Mail settings, the Rules pane’s help button opens the Mail User Guide on changing those settings, and if no topic fits, open the top level of the app’s help.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.027
Limit each window to a single help button. More than one in the same context makes a click’s outcome hard to predict.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.028
Put help buttons in the places people already look. Use the placements that follow.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.029
A dialog that includes dismissal buttons such as OK and Cancel places help in the lower corner opposite those buttons, lined up with them vertically.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.030
A dialog that lacks dismissal buttons places help at the lower-left or lower-right corner.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.031
A settings window or pane places help at the lower-left or lower-right corner.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.032
Keep the help button in the view itself, not the window frame, and leave it out of toolbars and status bars.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.033
Do not introduce a help button with extra text. Its job is already understood.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.034
Keep an image button in the view, not the window frame, and leave it out of toolbars and status bars. A toolbar that needs an image acting as a button should use a toolbar item.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.035
Leave about 10 pixels between the image boundary and the button boundary. The boundary is the clickable area even when it is invisible, and that inset keeps a slightly misplaced click effective; skip a system border on this kind of button in most cases.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.036
If you add a label, set it beneath the image button.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.037
A circular shape is available.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.038
A capsule that contains only text is available.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.039
A capsule that contains text plus an icon is available.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.040
A rounded rectangle is available.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.041
Favor buttons whose backdrop has a clear outline and a fill, since contrast makes them easier to spot. Toolbars, context menus, and alerts can omit that treatment when the parent’s shape and material already keep the control visible, and the notes below cover how it should look in several contexts.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.042
A button resting on a glass surface should use that glass material behind it.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.043
A button floating in space should use the background meant for that placement.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.044
Do not build a custom button from a white fill plus black lettering or icons. That combination is reserved for the toggled appearance.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.045
Circles and capsules are usually the better shapes, because corners pull the eyes and make a steady look at the center harder. The rounder the outline, the easier it is to keep watching it, and a lone button is best as a capsule.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.046
Separate buttons enough that looking at one is easy, with centers no closer than 60 pts. At 60 pts or above, surround each with 4 pts of padding so hover treatments do not collide, and generally skip small or mini sizes in a vertical stack or a horizontal line.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.047
For a vertical stack of text buttons, prefer a rounded rectangle; for a horizontal row, prefer a capsule.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.048
Standard controls bring audible feedback people already recognize, which matters especially on visionOS because haptics are not played.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.049
Corner buttons belong in a toolbar, which shifts the time and the title to make room and gives those buttons a distinct look against the content under them.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.050
Let a primary action stretch across the screen, since a full-width target looks better and is easier to tap. Two buttons sharing one row should match in height and each should use an image or a short title.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.051
Toolbar buttons should either move people toward a related area or expose a contextual action on the current content. They reach extra information or a secondary action for that content.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons

### buttons.052
In a vertical stack, keep one-line and two-line text buttons at one shared height so the stack stays visually consistent.
Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons
